Why this exists

Idle infrastructure is invisible until the invoice arrives

Nobody turns off a dev instance on a Friday. Nobody remembers the SageMaker notebook from a proof-of-concept three months ago. Multiply that across every team, every account, every region — and the waste stops being an anecdote and starts being a line item.

GreenOps is not another dashboard. It's a nightly decision engine: it reads real utilization data from Cost Explorer, checks every candidate resource against your tagging policy, and only acts on what's provably idle and explicitly safe to touch.

Every action is logged before it happens, reported to Slack in plain language, and reversible. The goal isn't to automate fear — it's to make the boring, obvious cleanup happen automatically, so your team can spend review time on the decisions that actually need a human.

01

Guardrails before actions

Nothing is stopped or terminated unless it carries AutoStop=true and isn't tagged Environment=prod. Policy is checked, not assumed.

02

Nightly, not manual

An EventBridge rule triggers the scan at 01:00 UTC — no dashboard to remember to check, no ticket to file.

03

Full audit trail

Every scan, decision, and estimated saving is written to DynamoDB before Slack ever sees it.

How a scan runs

Six steps, one Lambda invocation

The same sequence animating in the model above — hover a step here, or hover the model itself, to line them up.

01
EventBridgeScan
A scheduled rule fires nightly at 01:00 UTC, invoking the GreenOps Lambda across every tagged account and region.
02
IdleDetectorDetect
Each resource's recent CPU and network metrics are checked against idle thresholds before it's ever considered a candidate.
03
Cost ExplorerEstimate
Hourly pricing is pulled for the resource type and region, projecting the monthly savings if it's stopped.
04
Tag ValidatorGuardrail check
Only resources explicitly tagged safe to touch move forward — production and untagged resources are always skipped.
05
SlackNotifierNotify
A formatted summary — what was found, what was stopped, and what was saved — posts straight to the team channel.
06
DynamoDBAudit
Every decision is written to the audit log first, so nothing Slack reports can't be traced back and reviewed.
